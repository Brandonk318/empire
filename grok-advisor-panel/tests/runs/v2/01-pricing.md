# ADVISORY PACKET: Replace the $49 flat price with per-location tiers on March 1?
Mode: normal
Options: A) tiers for everyone March 1  B) tiers for new customers only  C) keep flat price, charge $25 for sales-tax filing
Brief gaps:
- Expected tier mix (how many accounts land in Starter / Pro / Group). Revenue under A and B depends on it.
- 8 of the 11 interviews aren't in the brief; only 3 quotes are visible to the panel.
- Cost to serve sales-tax filing (it's 22% of ticket volume, but the cost isn't given).
- Churn of the 164 tax-beta accounts vs non-users.

## SEAT: Customer Voice
Evidence base: 11 interviews (3 quotes visible), 0 calls, 0 tickets, 19 win/loss notes; from paying segment: yes
Position (this seat's lens only): Option C: the job customers describe paying for is tax filing, and the visible multi-location evidence pushes against per-location charging.
Points:
- [VERIFIED] 🟢 The job, in a customer's words: "the tax filing is the only reason I haven't gone back to my accountant" (Brief: interview, single location, Austin). Tax filing is what's being hired, not "bookkeeping".
- [VERIFIED] 🔴 Switching trigger against A: "Paying per location would hurt. We have four and two of them are tiny kiosks." (Brief: 4 locations, Denver). This is 1 of 11 interviews; we can't tell from the brief how many of the other 8 multi-location owners share it.
- [VERIFIED] 🟡 Pull towards paying more exists, but it's conditional: "I'd pay more if it actually handled multi-state." (Brief: 2 locations). The willingness is tied to a feature we don't have, not to the tier.
- [VERIFIED] 🟡 Who pays: lost-deal reasons split 7 price vs 6 missing POS integration out of 19 (Brief). Price is a factor in 7 ÷ 19 = 37% of losses, so a price rise for new customers (B) hits a known objection.
- [GAP] 🟡 The kiosk point suggests "location" isn't the unit customers think in (a kiosk isn't a restaurant). Ask the 8 unseen interviewees *How would you describe the size of your business?* to find the unit they'd accept.
Strongest argument against my position: Quotes from 3 of 11 interviews are a thin basis for rejecting a pricing model. The 78 multi-location accounts churn at only 1.4% (Brief), so they may complain and still stay.
What would change my mind: If 6+ of the 11 interviews show multi-location owners describing more locations as more work for them, which would make per-location billing feel fair.
Sources: Brief

## SEAT: Operator
Position (this seat's lens only): Option B: it changes billing for new signups only, so migration work and support spikes are avoided.
Points:
- [VERIFIED] 🟡 Billing already supports per-location (Brief), so A and B are configuration plus price-page work. The real work under A is migrating 412 accounts and handling the questions that follow.
- [ASSUMED] 🔴 First thing to break under A: support. Tax tickets are already 22% of volume (Brief). A price change for all 412 accounts adds a ticket wave in March, which is also US sales-tax filing season for many states.
- [ASSUMED] 🟡 Sequencing: under C, tax filing moves from free beta to paid. That needs a defined "what's included" list, or the 164 beta users (Brief) will open scope disputes.
- [ASSUMED] 🟢 Rollback: B is reversible per cohort; A's 6-month grandfathering (Brief) delays the point of no return to ~September but doesn't remove it.
Strongest argument against my position: B creates two price books to run indefinitely: legacy flat and tiered. That's a lasting support and billing complexity that A removes in 6 months.
What would change my mind: If support can show March ticket volume was under 1.2× the yearly average last year, the timing risk under A is smaller than I think.
Sources: Brief

## SEAT: CFO
Position (this seat's lens only): Option B: it captures the upside on new revenue without putting the low-churn multi-location base at risk.
Points:
- [VERIFIED] 🟢 Today's MRR ≈ 412 × $52 = $21,424 (Brief). Under A, if every location took Starter, MRR = 571 × $39 = $22,269 (+4%). If single-location accounts took Starter (334 × $39 = $13,026) and multi-location took Pro (237 × $79 = $18,723), MRR = $31,749 (+48%).
- [VERIFIED] 🔴 Bear case for A: the 78 multi-location accounts hold 237 locations (Brief), about 3.0 each. At Pro their bill goes from ~$52 to ~$240 (3.04 × $79), a 4.6× increase. If 25% of them leave [rough estimate], that's 20 accounts and up to ~$4,800 of the projected uplift lost, from the lowest-churn segment (1.4%/mo vs 3.1%).
- [VERIFIED] 🟡 Option C: 164 beta users × $25 = $4,100/mo (+19%) if all convert; if half convert, $2,050 (+10%) (Brief). That's a smaller but lower-variance gain.
- [GAP] 🟡 Payback can't be measured without the tier mix and the cost to serve tax filing. Gross margin is 81% overall (Brief), but tax filing may be the costly part.
Strongest argument against my position: B delays most of the revenue upside for years at 3.1% monthly churn, since the existing base turns over slowly. A's 6-month grandfathering already softens the bear case.
What would change my mind: If a sample of the 78 multi-location accounts shows that ≥ 70% would land in Starter rather than Pro, A's bear case shrinks to a ~2× bill increase.
Sources: Brief

## SEAT: Marketer
Position (this seat's lens only): Option A: one public price book, with tiers that name the multi-location value, is easier to sell than a flat price with a tax add-on.
Points:
- [VERIFIED] 🟡 Positioning against the real alternative, the accountant: the strongest value line in the brief is "the only reason I haven't gone back to my accountant" (Brief). Pro at $79 should be framed against the accountant's monthly fee, not against $49.
- [VERIFIED] 🔴 Demand signal: 7 of 19 lost deals cite price (Brief). Under A and B, a single-location buyer pays $39 (lower), but anyone wanting tax pays $79 (higher), so the tax buyer faces a $79 ÷ $49 = 1.61×, i.e. 61%, price rise.
- [ASSUMED] 🟡 Competitive response: a category incumbent can undercut a per-location price for multi-location groups with a flat "unlimited locations" offer. Group at $149/location is the most exposed tier.
- [GAP] 🟡 No data on which channels bring multi-location vs single-location signups, so tier messaging can't be targeted yet.
Strongest argument against my position: Raising the price of the most-loved feature (tax) during the period when 6 of 19 losses are about a missing POS integration means asking for more money before closing the main product gap.
What would change my mind: If a 4-week pricing-page test shows Pro conversion more than 30% below the current $49 conversion rate.
Sources: Brief

## SEAT: Customer Officer
Position (this seat's lens only): Option C: it keeps the multi-location base on predictable bills and charges for the feature that's driving the support load.
Points:
- [VERIFIED] 🔴 Revenue-at-risk map: the 78 multi-location accounts are 19% of logos but hold 237 of 571 locations (Brief) and churn least (1.4%/mo). A puts the steepest increase on the best-retaining segment.
- [GAP] 🔴 Gross vs net retention by segment isn't given. If the multi-location accounts pay legacy add-ons (ARPA $52 vs $49 list), part of A's "increase" is already priced in, and the risk is smaller.
- [ASSUMED] 🟡 Change cost under C: 164 beta users must accept a new $25 charge. The success plan needs 60 days' notice and a usage summary (*we filed X returns for you*) to justify it.
- [VERIFIED] 🟡 Under A, grandfathering for 6 months (Brief) moves the churn spike to month 7. The success team needs a named list of the 78 accounts and a call plan before month 5.
Strongest argument against my position: C charges the single-location tax users who called tax "the only reason" they stay. A paywall on the reason-to-stay could raise churn in the larger 3.1% segment.
What would change my mind: If tax-beta accounts churn at least 1 point lower than non-users, charging them is low-risk; if they churn higher, C is riskier than I'm assuming.
Sources: Brief

## SEAT: CEO
Position (this seat's lens only): Option B: it tests the pricing model on new revenue and keeps the option to move existing customers once the tier mix is known.
Points:
- [VERIFIED] 🟡 Opportunity cost: a full repricing (A) consumes Q1 attention while 6 of 19 losses point to the missing POS integration (Brief). The price fight competes with the product gap for the same quarter.
- [ASSUMED] 🟡 Three futures at 18 months: going well, tiers lift ARPA and Group becomes the multi-location wedge; as expected, most accounts settle in Starter and the gain is small; going badly, the multi-location base, the company's most loyal segment, is handed to a competitor.
- [ASSUMED] 🟢 Optionality: B and C are two-way doors; A is close to one-way for trust with existing customers. C also tests whether tax can stand as a product on its own.
Strongest argument against my position: Strategic clarity has value too. A single modern price book signals that Tallyhouse is built for groups, and B postpones that message for years.
What would change my mind: If the company's stated 2027 strategy is to win multi-location groups (not stated in the brief), A's positioning gain may justify the base risk.
Sources: Brief

- Tension: CFO vs Customer Voice on what customers pay for. CFO's upside comes from per-location charging; Customer Voice's evidence says the job is tax filing and "location" may be the wrong unit. Evidence that would separate them: the 8 unseen interviews, coded for the unit customers describe.

- Tension: Customer Officer vs Marketer on charging for tax (C). One sees fair payment for a costly feature, the other a 61% price rise on the most-loved feature. Evidence that would separate them: churn of tax-beta users vs non-users.
