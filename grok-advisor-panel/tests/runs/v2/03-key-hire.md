## SEAT: Operator
Position (this seat's lens only): Option C: one AE adds capacity without adding a management layer before there's a process to manage.
Points:
- [VERIFIED] 🔴 Capacity maths: the founder closes ~9 deals/month on a 19-day cycle (Brief). An AE ramping to half that by month 3 (ramp assumed) frees roughly 4–5 deals/month of founder time. That's the only option whose capacity gain doesn't depend on hiring a second layer.
- [ASSUMED] 🔴 First thing to break under A: the VP asks for 2 AE reqs within 6 months (Brief), so the founder stays the closer for months 1–3 while also onboarding a VP. Founder time is the bottleneck, and it gets worse before it gets better.
- [GAP] 🟡 No written sales process (stages, qualification, handoff to onboarding) is described. Under B, two AEs learn from a founder who hasn't written it down, which doubles the inconsistency.
- [ASSUMED] 🟢 Rollback: C is the easiest to undo (one hire, 90-day review). A is the hardest: senior-hire exit cost plus 1.2% equity (Brief) already vesting.
Strongest argument against my position: A experienced VP writes the sales process in month 1; C leaves the founder as the process owner indefinitely, which is the actual bottleneck.
What would change my mind: If the founder can show 15+ qualified opportunities per month going unworked today, the capacity gap is bigger than one AE can fill.
Sources: Brief

## SEAT: CFO
Position (this seat's lens only): Option C: lowest added fixed cost while cash is unknown.
Points:
- [GAP] 🔴 Runway can't be computed: cash is TBD (Brief). Scenarios at today's $68k/mo burn: cash $500k → 7.4 months; $1.0M → 14.7 months; $1.5M → 22.1 months.
- [VERIFIED] 🔴 Burn multiple today: $68,000 net burn ÷ ($2,600 × 12 = $31,200 net new ARR added per month) = 2.2 (Brief). Under A, burn rises by $185,000 ÷ 12 = $15,417/mo base, so the multiple is 2.7 until the VP adds new ARR.
- [VERIFIED] 🟡 One-off recruiter fees: A $37,000 (20% × $185k); B $28,000 (20% × $140k); C $14,000 (Brief). At $500k cash, A's fee plus base uses 1.4 months of runway in the first quarter.
- [VERIFIED] 🟡 Payback bar: the VP's at-target cost of $245,000/yr ($185k + $60k) ÷ 12 = $20,417/mo is 95% of today's $21,400 MRR (Brief). To pay back within 12 months, the VP's team must add about $20k MRR, roughly 8× the current monthly net-new rate.
- [ASSUMED] 🟡 Bear case for B: two AEs at $70k base ramp slower than planned (6 months, not 3). That adds $11,667/mo in base with little new MRR for two quarters, a burn multiple near 2.6.
Strongest argument against my position: If cash is $1.5M+, C's savings are small, and the cost of the founder staying the bottleneck for 6 more months may exceed the VP's salary.
What would change my mind: Cash ≥ $1.3M (about 15 months of runway after A's added cost and fee), combined with a credible plan to bring the burn multiple under 2.0 within 3 quarters.
Sources: Brief

## SEAT: Marketer
Position (this seat's lens only): Option B: two AEs double the number of conversations; positioning credibility comes from the founder's story, not a VP title.
Points:
- [VERIFIED] 🟡 The brief's only buyer signal is restaurant credibility (Brief: customer evidence). An ex-Toast regional director brings category credibility, not operator credibility. Whether buyers count those as the same is untested.
- [GAP] 🟡 Demand signal by channel is missing: we don't know whether the ~9 closes/month come from inbound, referral or founder outreach. If they're inbound, extra AEs convert more of the existing demand; if they're founder outreach, demand may not survive the handoff.
- [ASSUMED] 🟢 Two AEs can split by segment (single-location vs multi-location) and give a clean A/B on messaging within one quarter. A VP's first quarter is typically spent hiring, not testing.
Strongest argument against my position: Two junior AEs with no playbook can damage the founder-led reputation in a word-of-mouth market faster than one senior hire can.
What would change my mind: If more than 60% of last quarter's closes came from founder-originated outreach rather than inbound or referral, the handoff risk makes B weaker.
Sources: Brief

## SEAT: Customer Officer
Position (this seat's lens only): Option C: protect onboarding quality for new accounts while sales capacity grows slowly.
Points:
- [GAP] 🔴 Retention by acquisition source isn't in the brief. If founder-sold accounts retain better than self-serve, a handoff to any non-founder seller is a churn risk that should be measured first.
- [ASSUMED] 🟡 Change cost for new customers: whoever sells sets expectations. Faster closing (B) without a defined handoff to onboarding tends to produce first-90-day churn. The 19-day cycle (Brief) leaves little slack.
- [GAP] 🟡 Who owns onboarding today isn't stated. If it's also the founder, every option adds load at the point after the sale.
Strongest argument against my position: Slower sales growth is also a retention problem. A company adding ~$2,600 net MRR a month (Brief) can't fund a proper CS function at all.
What would change my mind: If 90-day logo retention for founder-sold accounts is within 2 points of non-founder-sold accounts, the handoff risk is small.
Sources: Brief

## SEAT: CEO
Position (this seat's lens only): Option A: the scarce resource is founder time, and only A gives it back for good.
Points:
- [VERIFIED] 🟡 Opportunity cost: the founder "runs all sales today" (Brief). Every month that stays true is a month the founder isn't on product, fundraising or partnerships. B and C keep the founder as sales manager.
- [ASSUMED] 🟡 Three futures at 18 months under A: going well, the VP has built a 3-person team and the founder is out of the deal flow; as expected, the VP hires AEs and the founder still closes the large deals; going badly, the VP leaves at month 9 with 1.2% equity partly vested and the team half built.
- [ASSUMED] 🔴 One-way door: a senior hire with equity is the least reversible option on the table. C keeps the VP door open in 6 months with better data.
- [GAP] 🟡 The one main bet for the next 12 months isn't stated in the brief. If it's product depth (e.g. tax), adding a sales leader optimises the wrong constraint.
Strongest argument against my position: Hiring a VP before the founder has a repeatable motion often means paying senior salary to discover there's no playbook. C buys that discovery cheaply.
What would change my mind: If cash turns out to be under ~12 months of runway after the hire, or if the founder can't name 3 things they'd do with 20 freed hours a week.
